Title: High-Throughput Transformer Architectures for Dense Retrieval
Authors: Dr. Evelyn Reed, Marcus Vance, Sophia Chen
Affiliations: Institute for Artificial Intelligence, Stanford University; Google DeepMind
Publication Year: 2024
Abstract: We present HyperDense, a novel neural architecture designed for low-latency dense document retrieval. Our method delivers outstanding scalability and achieves state-of-the-art results across standard information retrieval benchmarks.

1. Introduction and Objectives
Dense semantic vector retrieval has revolutionized modern NLP search systems. However, traditional dual-encoder architectures frequently encounter significant memory bottlenecks during multi-document indexing. Our objective is to design a lightweight projection network that accelerates semantic search without compromising retrieval accuracy.

2. Methods and Experimental Framework
We evaluate our approach using a 12-layer transformer encoder pre-trained on the Common Crawl corpus. All experiments were conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs using PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers. The learning rate was set to 1e-4 with an AdamW optimizer and linear decay schedule.

3. Benchmark Results and Findings
Experimental results demonstrate remarkable gains across all evaluated metrics. On the SQuAD-v2 benchmark, HyperDense achieves an impressive accuracy of 93.8% and macro F1 of 91.2%, significantly outperforming the baseline BERT and RoBERTa models by 4.2 points. Furthermore, query latency is reduced by 60%, delivering an outstanding throughput of 1,200 queries per second with zero loss in fidelity.

4. Conclusion
HyperDense sets a new benchmark for scalable, high-accuracy document retrieval. Future work will extend this framework to multilingual and cross-lingual question answering.
